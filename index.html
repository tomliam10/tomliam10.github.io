The only 3 things worth buying first

You don't need a whole cabinet of gear to cook Thai food well. These are the three that actually change the result.

The non-negotiable

A granite mortar & pestle (krok)

Curry pastes and som tum dressing depend on pounding, not blending — it bruises the aromatics instead of just cutting them, which changes the flavor. A 5–6" granite krok is the standard size for home cooking.

Quick answers

Thai food, explained

Is Pad Thai actually the most popular dish in Thailand?

Not quite — it's Thailand's most famous export dish, promoted as a national dish starting in the 1930s–40s, but everyday home cooking leans more on curries, stir-fries, and regional dishes like som tum and larb.

What's the one ingredient I shouldn't skip?

Fish sauce. It's the backbone of savory flavor in most Thai dishes — there's no substitute that gets you the same result.

Do I need a wok to cook Thai food at home?

It helps but isn't required for everything — curries and soups work fine in a regular pot. Stir-fried noodle dishes like Pad Thai benefit the most from a wok's high heat.

Where can I buy Thai ingredients near me?

An Asian or Southeast Asian grocery store is your best bet for fresh herbs and the best prices. See our full guide for what to look for.
