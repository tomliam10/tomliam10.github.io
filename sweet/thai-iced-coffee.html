Thai Iced Coffee (Oliang)

Thailand's answer to iced coffee — smoky, slightly sweet, and made from a very different bean mix than what you'd expect.

Thai iced coffee photo goes here
What makes it different

Not just coffee over ice

Oliang (โอเลี้ยง) is traditionally made from a mix of roasted Robusta coffee grounds blended with other roasted grains and seeds — corn, sesame, soybeans, sometimes cardamom — rather than 100% coffee. That blend gives it a smokier, more complex aroma than a straight iced coffee, closer to a roasted grain drink with coffee in it than the reverse.

Like Thai iced tea, it's traditionally sweetened with sugar and finished with condensed or evaporated milk poured over ice — though it's also completely fine unsweetened and black, which is closer to how it's often served at Thai street stalls.

Recipe

How to make it at home

Prep5 min
Brew5 min
Serves1
HeatNone (served cold)

Ingredients

  • 3 tbsp oliang mix (or strong dark-roast coffee grounds if unavailable)
  • 1 cup hot water
  • 1-2 tbsp sweetened condensed milk, to taste
  • Ice
  • Splash of evaporated milk, for topping (optional)

Steps

  1. Brew strong. Brew oliang mix (or strong coffee) with hot water using a cloth filter, French press, or drip method — it needs to be concentrated since it's about to hit ice.
  2. Sweeten while hot. Stir in condensed milk while the coffee is still hot so it dissolves fully. Adjust to taste.
  3. Cool slightly. Let it come down from boiling before pouring over ice, so the ice doesn't melt too fast and water it down.
  4. Pour over ice. Fill a glass with ice and pour the sweetened coffee over it.
  5. Top with milk (optional). Pour evaporated milk over the back of a spoon so it sits as a layer on top rather than mixing straight in.

Notes

True oliang mix is sold at most Asian grocery stores or online — it's what gives the smoky, grain-forward flavor that separates it from a regular iced coffee. If you only have coffee grounds on hand, it'll still taste good, just closer to a standard iced coffee than the traditional drink.

Want the milkier, tea-based version instead? Try Thai Iced Tea.
