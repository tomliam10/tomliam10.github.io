Mango Sticky Rice (Khao Niew Mamuang)

Sweet, salty coconut sauce over warm sticky rice, next to cold ripe mango. Simple ingredients, but the ratios matter more than people think.

Prep4 hr (soak)
Cook25 min
Serves4
HeatNone

Why this recipe works

This dish lives or dies on two things: using actual glutinous ("sticky") rice — not just any short-grain rice — and getting the coconut sauce salty enough. Most home versions skip the salt in the sauce, which is what actually makes it taste balanced next to sweet mango instead of just cloying.

Ingredients

  • 1.5 cups Thai glutinous (sticky) rice
  • 1.5 cups coconut milk, divided
  • 1/2 cup sugar
  • 1/2 tsp salt
  • 2 ripe mangoes (Nam Dok Mai or Ataulfo work well)
  • 1 tbsp toasted mung beans or sesame seeds, for topping (optional)

Steps

  1. Soak the rice. Cover glutinous rice in water and soak at least 4 hours, or overnight. This isn't optional — unsoaked sticky rice won't steam evenly.
  2. Steam the rice. Drain and steam over boiling water in a cloth-lined steamer or bamboo basket for 20-25 minutes, until translucent and tender.
  3. Make the sauce. While rice steams, warm 1 cup coconut milk with sugar and salt in a saucepan over low heat, stirring until dissolved. Don't boil — just warm through.
  4. Combine. Pour about two-thirds of the warm coconut sauce over the hot steamed rice. Stir gently and let sit 10-15 minutes so the rice absorbs it.
  5. Make the topping sauce. Warm the remaining coconut milk with a pinch of salt separately — this stays thinner and gets drizzled on top at serving, not mixed in.
  6. Slice the mango. Peel and slice ripe mango just before serving, so it doesn't oxidize or turn watery.
  7. Serve. Rice and mango side by side, topping sauce drizzled over both, mung beans or sesame scattered on top.

Notes

Mango ripeness is the one thing you can't fix with technique — underripe mango stays firm and starchy no matter what you do. Look for mango that gives slightly under gentle pressure and smells sweet at the stem.

Want something to make alongside it? Try Thai Iced Tea — the two are a classic street-food pairing.
