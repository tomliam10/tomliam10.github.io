Pad Thai, Done Right

The dish everyone's had a bad version of. Here's the sauce ratio, the noodle prep, and the order things hit the wok — the three things that actually separate good Pad Thai from the sweet, ketchup-red version.

Prep25 min
Cook10 min
Serves2
HeatAdjustable

Why this recipe works

Most Pad Thai that disappoints was built backwards: ketchup and sugar standing in for tamarind, and everything crammed into the wok at once so the noodles steam instead of fry. This version fixes both — a proper tamarind-fish sauce-palm sugar base, and a strict order of operations so the noodles stay separate and slightly chewy instead of clumping into mush.

Ingredients

Sauce

  • 3 tbsp tamarind paste (concentrate, not sweetened)
  • 3 tbsp fish sauce
  • 3 tbsp palm sugar, grated (or brown sugar)
  • 1 tbsp lime juice

Noodles & stir-fry

  • 7 oz dried flat rice noodles (banh pho width)
  • 2 tbsp neutral oil
  • 8 oz shrimp or chicken, or 6 oz firm tofu
  • 2 eggs
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 cup bean sprouts, plus extra raw for serving
  • 3 scallions, cut into 2-inch pieces
  • 2 tbsp dried shrimp, chopped (optional)
  • 3 tbsp roasted peanuts, crushed
  • Lime wedges and chili flakes, to serve

Steps

  1. Soak the noodles. Cover dried rice noodles in room-temperature water and let sit 25–30 minutes, until pliable but still firm — they'll finish cooking in the wok. Drain well.
  2. Make the sauce. Whisk tamarind paste, fish sauce, palm sugar, and lime juice in a small bowl until the sugar mostly dissolves. Taste — it should hit sour, salty, and sweet all at once. Set aside.
  3. Prep everything before you turn on the heat. Pad Thai moves fast once it starts; have the protein, garlic, eggs, sprouts, scallions, and sauce all within arm's reach.
  4. Sear the protein. Heat oil in a wok or wide skillet over high heat. Add garlic, then protein, and stir-fry until just cooked through. Push to one side of the wok.
  5. Scramble the egg. Crack eggs into the cleared space, scramble briefly, then fold into the protein.
  6. Add the noodles and sauce. Add drained noodles and the sauce. Toss constantly with tongs for 2–3 minutes until noodles are tender and have absorbed the sauce — add a splash of water if they look dry before they're fully cooked.
  7. Finish. Add bean sprouts, scallions, and dried shrimp. Toss for 30 seconds more, just to warm through and keep the sprouts crisp.
  8. Serve immediately topped with crushed peanuts, extra raw bean sprouts, a lime wedge, and chili flakes on the side so everyone can adjust heat themselves.

Notes

Tamarind concentrate varies a lot in strength by brand — always taste and adjust before adding it to the wok. If you can't find tamarind, a mix of lime juice and a splash of rice vinegar gets you closer than skipping it entirely. For the pan itself, a carbon steel wok gets hotter than a nonstick skillet and makes a real difference here. For fish sauce, we cook with Three Crabs or Megachef — both cost more than the bottom-shelf bottles, and the difference actually comes through in the final dish.
