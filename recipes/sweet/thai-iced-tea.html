Thai Iced Tea (Cha Yen)

The bright orange, milky-sweet iced tea served everywhere from Thai street carts to American Thai restaurants. Here's what's actually in it, where the color comes from, and how to make it at home.

What it actually is

Not flavored with fruit or spice

Cha yen means, literally, "cold tea" in Thai. It's brewed from strong black tea — traditionally Ceylon or Assam — sweetened with sugar and condensed milk, then poured over ice and finished with a top layer of evaporated milk that's meant to be swirled in right before drinking, not stirred in ahead of time.

The one thing that surprises most people: the vivid orange color isn't from a spice blend or a special tea plant. Brewed black tea on its own is a deep reddish-brown. Most commercial Thai tea mixes get their signature bright orange from food coloring — and the "warm spice" flavor some Western versions lean on (cinnamon, star anise, cardamom) isn't part of the traditional street-cart recipe at all; that's largely a flavor added by cafes outside Thailand.

Where it came from

A cash crop that became a street drink

Thailand pushed tea cultivation in the north starting in the 1980s, originally as a way to replace opium poppy farming with a legal cash crop. Street vendors standardized a version built for volume and heat — strong tea, ice, condensed milk — and it spread from local stalls to becoming one of the country's most recognizable exports, showing up on nearly every Thai restaurant menu abroad today.

Recipe

How to make it at home

Prep10 min
Steep10 min
Serves2
HeatNone (served cold)

Ingredients

  • 1/4 cup Thai tea mix (or strong black tea/Ceylon)
  • 2 cups hot water
  • 3-4 tbsp sweetened condensed milk, to taste
  • Ice
  • 2-4 tbsp evaporated milk, for topping

Steps

  1. Steep the tea. Pour hot water over the tea mix and steep 5-10 minutes for a strong, dark brew — it needs to be concentrated since it's about to be diluted with ice and milk.
  2. Strain. Strain out the tea leaves through a fine sieve or cloth strainer into a pitcher.
  3. Sweeten. Stir in condensed milk while the tea is still hot, to taste. Let cool to room temperature.
  4. Pour over ice. Fill a glass with ice and pour the sweetened tea over it.
  5. Finish with the milk layer. Slowly pour evaporated milk over the back of a spoon so it floats as a distinct layer on top instead of mixing in — this is the visual signature of the drink. Stir just before drinking, not before.

Notes

Real Thai tea mix (Cha Tra Mue, Pantai, or Number One brand) gives you the closest flavor and color to what you'd get from a street cart — a plain black tea works, but the flavor profile shifts slightly. Skip artificial neon-orange mixes if you'd rather avoid added dye; the tea will read more amber than bright orange, but the flavor holds up fine.

Pairs naturally with mango sticky rice — a very common street-food combination.
