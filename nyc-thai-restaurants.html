Top 5 Thai Restaurants in New York City

New York's Thai food scene runs from Isaan street food in Queens to Michelin-recognized kitchens in Manhattan. These five are worth the trip right now — with the dish to order at each.

A research roundup, not a first-hand review: we haven't personally eaten at every spot on this list yet. Picks are cross-referenced from Yelp, Time Out New York, and local food press — not a taste-tested ranking like our recipes. Last checked August 2026.

Photo from a visit goes here
01
WILLIAMSBURG, BROOKLYN · $$$

Kru

Named the best Thai restaurant in NYC by Time Out, Kru comes from husband-and-wife chefs reworking century-old Thai recipes with bold, unapologetic spice. It's earned a James Beard nomination and Michelin recognition in its short run.

Order: Ma Hor (sweet-and-spicy fruit bites) and the beef tongue massaman curry — both called out as the kitchen's standouts.

02
WOODSIDE, QUEENS · $$

SriPraPhai

The name New Yorkers reach for when they mean it — a longstanding Queens destination that draws crowds without needing any hype. Best approached with a group so you can order broadly across the menu.

Order: Crispy watercress salad and the whole fried fish, both menu staples regulars build a table around.

Add photo
03
EAST VILLAGE, MANHATTAN · $$

Somtum Der

An Isaan specialist that brings the bright, sour energy of Northeastern Thailand to the East Village — loud, colorful, and built around the region's signature salads.

Order: Som Tum (green papaya salad) — it's the restaurant's namesake and reason for being.

Add photo
04
EAST VILLAGE, MANHATTAN · $$

Soothr

A Bangkok street-market feel with modern-meets-traditional decor. Known for curries with real depth rather than the thin, sweet versions common elsewhere.

Order: Koong Karee (shrimp in a creamy curry sauce) and the Chicken Khao Soi for its mix of crispy and chewy noodle textures.

Add photo
05
UPPER EAST SIDE, MANHATTAN · $$$

Up Thai

A small, Michelin-recognized Upper East Side spot with a focus on Northern Thai cooking — an intimate room that punches well above its size.

Order: Kow Muni Gai (steamed chicken over ginger rice) and the Pad See Ew, plus mango sticky rice to finish.
